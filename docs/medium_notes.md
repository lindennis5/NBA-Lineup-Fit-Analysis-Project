# Future article notes — no final article yet

Working framing: **Does NBA Lineup Fit Actually Matter? Testing Whether Teams Should Fill Their Biggest Weakness**

- Hook: a fifth player can be excellent individually and still duplicate a core's strengths. The project asks whether the alternative produces a measurable difference.
- Explain the four-plus-one comparison with five name cards, then reveal that every five-player unit creates five views of the same minutes. Five views do not mean five independent experiments.
- Feasibility story: a plausible league-wide API response returned only 500 lineups. The most important early result was discovering truncation before modeling.
- Explain gap closure as comparing the four-player average with the five-player average, with no reward once a below-average gap is filled.
- Explain talent control as a prior-season production proxy, including what it misses.
- Candidate evidence: possession distribution; threshold tradeoff; one high-exposure core with several fifth players selected without looking at outcomes; effect/uncertainty plot.
- Reserve the headline conclusion until external validation and robustness are adequate. An inconclusive result may mean fit is small, the proxy is weak, talent is poorly measured, or observational context obscures the signal.
- Do not invent anecdotes, novelty claims, sample sizes, or final resume bullets.
